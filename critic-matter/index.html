Abstract

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) trains a few-step student using the difference between a teacher's score and an online critic's estimate of the student distribution's score. We investigate how critic capacity and conditioning affect distillation. Controlled experiments across seven SiT model sizes on ImageNet show that increasing teacher size while keeping a small critic fixed can degrade student quality and cause training collapse. Scaling the critic alongside the teacher, which we call joint scaling, allows the student to benefit from larger teachers. We also introduce a step-aware critic (STAC), which conditions on the student's generation step to estimate step-specific scores. Under a pure distribution-matching objective, joint scaling and STAC together reduce the FID of a 4-step SiT-B student from 13.91 to 6.90 on ImageNet without CFG. We further validate our findings on text-to-image and text-to-video generation with SDXL, FLUX.2, and Wan2.1. Additionally, we explore critic designs and training strategies, with comprehensive results and ablations reported in the appendix.

Open questions about critic design

We study two independent questions about the online critic. Within each comparison, the deployed student keeps the same size and number of sampling steps.

1How large should the critic be?

Scale it together with the teacher.

Scaling the teacher while keeping a small critic fixed can degrade the student and even collapse training. Scaling the critic alongside the teacher, which we call joint scaling, lets the same student benefit from larger teachers, and matched teacher–critic pairs offer a favorable quality–compute tradeoff.

2What should the critic be conditioned on?

The critic should know the generation step.

A multi-step student produces clean predictions with different characteristics at each generation step. A standard critic sees the noise level t but not the generation step k, so it estimates the score of their mixture. Our step-aware critic (STAC) also conditions on k to estimate each step's own score, with no added inference cost.
